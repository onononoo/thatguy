    how it works

    every coin has a public api that shows what's happening on its blockchain right now. thatguy asks one of them for a recent block, a page of transactions, or a slice of the account list, and picks an address out of it. one answer holds dozens of addresses, so the rest are kept in this browser for 15 minutes and most refreshes never touch the network.

    tokens like tether, usd coin, chainlink and shiba inu don't have addresses of their own, so thatguy finds people who just sent or received them on ethereum, tron or solana. smart contracts are filtered out, so what's left are ordinary wallets.

    monero is the one exception. its blockchain hides who sends and who receives, so there are no real addresses to find. monero addresses here are made up, with a valid checksum and valid keys, but nobody owns them.

    some apis have small free limits. if one says no, the page shows a made-up address instead and says so rather than pretending. please don't send anything to any of these addresses: the real ones belong to strangers, and the made-up ones belong to nobody.
